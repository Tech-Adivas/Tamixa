## North star (one line)

**Trusted Indian family audio: Fun for the heart, Edu for real life—always story-first.**

---

## 1. Brand and product values

| Value | What it means |
|-------|----------------|
| **Story first, lesson second** | Every Edu piece must work as a real story; learning is woven in, not bolted on. |
| **Trust over hype** | Especially for money, online safety, and family topics: calm, accurate tone; no fear-mongering; no disguised selling. |
| **Transparent growth and support** | Marketing and product copy match real operations (e.g. no predatory outbound sales; human help when the user asks—if policy changes, update UI immediately). See [EDUSTORY_INDIA_PROBLEMS_AND_FEATURES.md](EDUSTORY_INDIA_PROBLEMS_AND_FEATURES.md) §9. |
| **Respect every age** | Same themes may span kids through seniors; voice and stakes change, dignity does not. |
| **Indian reality, many languages** | Joint family, UPI-era life, exams, regional settings—not a generic Western default. Mother-tongue depth is a value, not a checkbox. |
| **Fun and Edu, clearly labeled** | Fun builds habit and wonder; Edu builds judgment and vocabulary. Users always know which track they chose. |
| **Family in the loop** | Encourage co-listening and short conversation where helpful; the product is household infrastructure, not only solo kid time. |
| **Safety and child wellbeing** | Strict moderation, age-appropriate framing, no shame-based “lessons.” |
| **Human warmth + optional tech magic** | Curated quality and real voices matter; AI, voice clone, and avatar serve bonding and access, not novelty alone. |
| **Honesty about AI and voice** | Clear communication so families associate Tamixa with ethics and protection, not confusion with scams or deepfakes. |
| **Editorial independence** | Sponsors, schools, and CSR funders must not rewrite stories into ads. Partnerships expand reach; they do not compromise the message. |

## 2. User-facing value (what we promise)

| Stakeholder | Promise |
|-------------|---------|
| **Parents** | “I trust what they hear”; life skills without nagging; our languages and values; optional narration in a family member’s voice. |
| **Kids and teens** | Stories that feel real or cool, not homework; agency (choices, reflection) without grading people into shame. |
| **Household** | Shared vocabulary (money, safety, boundaries) from the same app used for Fun. |
| **Schools / CSR (when used)** | Proven, safe IP plus simple facilitation; impact reporting without creepy child-level surveillance; alignment with digital safety and life-skills goals—without claiming formal curriculum certification unless explicitly obtained. |

## 3. Operational values (how we ship)

- **Clarity** — One sentence per series: intent, suggested age band, primary language(s).
- **Consistency** — Fun and Edu share the same bar for audio quality and safety review.
- **Measure what matters** — Completion, return next week, replay of one chapter (discussion), not only raw vanity metrics that punish reflection-first Edu.
- **Privacy by design** — Especially for institutional use: aggregate reporting, minimal PII, explicit consent for anything identifiable.
- **Boundaries on sensitive topics** — Edu fiction is **not** therapy, legal advice, investment advice, or medical advice. See [EDUSTORY_INDIA_PROBLEMS_AND_FEATURES.md](EDUSTORY_INDIA_PROBLEMS_AND_FEATURES.md) §Safeguards.

## 4. Partnership rules (B2C + school / CSR later)

- **Badges and “verification”** — Any **third-party** or **“certified”** life-readiness language requires **written** agreements and accurate scope ([EDUSTORY_PRODUCT_VISION.md](EDUSTORY_PRODUCT_VISION.md) §3.9.4). Do not imply NGO or employer endorsement without contract.
- **Contracts** — Editorial independence clause; no product placement disguised as Edu episodes unless explicitly labeled commercial content (separate from core Edu lane).
- **Institutional packs** — Same narrative IP can ship as “family edition” and “facilitator edition” (shorter hooks, discussion guide); one production spine, two surfaces.
- **Metrics for renewals** — Prefer anonymized aggregates and optional surveys (“felt more confident about X”) over per-child behavioral tracking as a default.
